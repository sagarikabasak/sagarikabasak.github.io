About

Connecting analytical structure to quantum dynamics.

My research asks a common question across open systems and quantum matter: how can we describe, control, and learn from interacting quantum systems away from equilibrium?

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Center for Quantum Research and Technology at the University of Oklahoma, working with Prof. Doerte Blume. I earned my Ph.D. in Physics from Rice University with Prof. Han Pu, where I developed efficient many-body frameworks for strongly interacting spinor gases and multicomponent anyons.

Across these problems, I combine theory, high-performance numerical methods, and close attention to experimentally accessible observables. I am especially interested in frameworks that remain accurate when familiar approximations become restrictive.

Research

Four connected directions

From reduced descriptions of driven systems to emergent phases of quantum matter, each direction is grounded in interpretable theory and computationally useful methods.

01

Driven open quantum systems

Developing invariant-based master equations that retain the effect of strong, time-dependent driving in dissipation. Current work extends the framework from two-level systems to driven qutrits, multilevel ladders, structured reservoirs, and leaky cavities.

  • Lewis–Riesenfeld invariants
  • Structured baths
  • Atom–cavity systems
02

Ultracold quantum matter

Studying strongly correlated bosons and fermions in optical lattices, from coupled Bose gases and effective spin models to transport in the one-dimensional Hubbard model.

  • Optical lattices
  • Spin and charge transport
  • Hubbard models
03

Anyonic systems

Building efficient descriptions of multicomponent and composite anyons, benchmarking generalized effective spin-chain methods, and exploring statistical-phase sensitivity for quantum metrology.

  • Multicomponent anyons
  • Composite quasiparticles
  • Quantum metrology
04

Quantum dynamics & computation

Combining analytical theory with exact diagonalization, tensor-network methods, variational Monte Carlo, and open-system numerics to make complex quantum dynamics tractable and experimentally interpretable.

  • DMRG & TDVP
  • Exact diagonalization
  • Variational Monte Carlo
